A different approach to sterilisation

The product stays packaged. The process stays measurable.

Gamma irradiation can be applied to products in their final packaging. Process assurance is centred on absorbed-dose measurement and validation—not on assuming one dose fits every product.

  1. Receive, identify and review.Products arrive with their batch identity and condition checked. Product family, packaging format and material compatibility are reviewed.
  2. Prepare the product for its batch.The product is prepared according to the batch instruction. Arrangement and loading density are considered because they influence dose distribution.
  3. Load and identify each container.Prepared products are placed in irradiation containers and linked to a batch identity before entering the conveyor route.
  4. Run the controlled irradiation cycle.After safety readiness is confirmed, the source rack is raised from the water pool and containers circulate around it. The source returns to its shielded storage position after the cycle.
  5. Verify the dose received by the product.Product dosimetry assesses absorbed-dose distribution across the batch against the validated process range. This is separate from worker radiation monitoring.
  6. Complete the record and dispatch.After process review and documentation, the batch moves to post-irradiation storage and dispatch.

04 / Radiation protection

Radiation protection

Safety is not a production target. It is the condition for operation.

The management policy in the concept places radiation safety ahead of output. The Radiation Protection Officer and Operations Supervisor are assigned authority to stop operation whenever a safety function cannot be demonstrated to work.

A non-negotiable principleNo bypass of interlocks, alarms or radiation monitors.

Access interlocks

Personnel and product access routes are controlled and linked to safety functions.

Radiation monitoring

Fixed and portable instruments support area monitoring and output checks.

Source & pool control

Source position, pool level, water condition and contamination are monitored.

People & readiness

Training, personal dosimetry, inspections and emergency exercises form the management framework.

Emergency preparedness / High-level framework

Prepared to detect, coordinate and recover.

The academic PPKR includes fifteen facility emergency scenarios and an eight-part preparedness programme. The summary below explains the management logic; it is not an emergency instruction or a substitute for approved procedures.

01

Recognise & report

Alarm, monitor and abnormal-condition reporting support early recognition. Scenarios include loss of safety function, container jam, pool-level abnormality, fire and abnormal exposure.

02

Coordinate & protect people

The response organisation assigns responsibilities and communication. Personnel dosimetry, access control and support from competent, authorised specialists remain central.

03

Verify, recover & learn

Recovery follows confirmation of a safe source condition, surveys, repair and safety verification. The programme then records findings, corrective actions and lessons learned.

Source or rack recovery is not depicted as a manual task in this concept. The source document assigns specialised recovery work to competent and authorised personnel in coordination with the manufacturer, the Radiation Protection Officer and the regulator.

06 / Quality & process assurance

A validated process. A documented batch.

Product quality and radiation protection are connected, but they are not the same control system. Product dosimetry assesses absorbed dose in the product; worker monitoring assesses occupational exposure.

08 / Technical language

A shared technical language.

A concise guide to the terms used throughout this concept.

Gamma irradiation How the process works

A process in which ionising gamma radiation is used to treat a product. In this concept the source radionuclide is Cobalt-60.

Wet-storage Source storage arrangement

The source rack is lowered into a water-filled storage pool when irradiation is not taking place. The water provides shielding in the storage position.

Absorbed dose Product process quantity

Energy deposited per unit mass, expressed in gray (Gy). Product process doses may be expressed in kilogray (kGy).

Interlock A safety-related permissive

An arrangement that prevents a function—such as raising the source rack—from proceeding unless required safety conditions are fulfilled. No bypass is represented or encouraged.

ALARA Radiation protection principle

As Low As Reasonably Achievable: exposures are kept as low as reasonably achievable, taking relevant factors into account.

Product dosimetry Not worker dosimetry

Measurement of absorbed dose in the product to assess the irradiation process. It is distinct from personal dose monitoring of radiation workers.

09 / Frequently asked

Clear answers. Clear boundaries.

Is KIM currently operating?

No. KIM and the facility described here are a fictional academic scenario. This website does not represent an operating company, licensed facility or commercial service.

Is the 3D model an engineering design?

No. It is a conceptual reconstruction based on the room relationships and process diagrams in PPKR-KIM-001/2026. It is not to scale and is not an as-built or construction drawing.

Does every medical product use 25 kGy?

No. The 25 kGy figure in the academic brief is illustrative. The actual process dose must be established by product-specific validation and quality controls.

What does the simulator show?

It illustrates facility relationships, product transport, source storage, pool-water conditioning and conceptual safety functions. It does not calculate radiation fields or provide operating instructions.
